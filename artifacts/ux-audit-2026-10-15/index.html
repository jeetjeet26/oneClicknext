Priority order

Fix trust and clarity first.

High findings can block a task or distort understanding. Medium findings create recurring friction or access risk. Severity reflects expert judgment, not measured user failure rates.

F01 · High

Turn raw intelligence into a useful answer

Property intelligence renders a long sequence of ratios, coverage explanations and competitor observations. Repeated dates, unknown values and source links have nearly the same visual weight as useful results.

Why it matters: Users must interpret the records themselves and cannot quickly tell what changed or what needs attention.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Lead with a short takeaway, 3–4 meaningful results and one next action. Show only 3–5 market observations, grouped for comparison, with a link to MarketVision. Keep data completeness visible and put supporting methodology in an expandable detail view.

Check: A first-time user identifies the property, period, main change and next action without scrolling through raw records.

F02 · High

Make Community knowledge a review workflow

Sources, text chunks, search preparation, publication history, file processing and large entry forms compete on the main surface. Existing approved knowledge is not the first thing users see.

Why it matters: A property manager has to understand the storage and processing model before updating a policy.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Show current facts and a Needs review queue first. Guide Add source → Review suggested information → Approve. Keep processing history and advanced controls available to administrators. Only claim a fact is used by an assistant when that state is confirmed.

Check: A user finds a pet policy, sees its source and approval state, and updates it without needing to understand text chunks or search preparation.

F03 · High

Repair links and confusing destinations

Review brand sources opens /dashboard/brandforge and produces a 404. Luma AI Assistant redirects to the leasing conversation inbox. The portfolio page keeps a property selector that does not select its portfolio.

Why it matters: Users lose confidence and cannot predict what a navigation item will do.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Fix the brand link, remove or accurately rename the duplicate assistant destination, and give portfolio work an explicit multi-property context. Use task names before product brands.

Check: Every main link opens the promised task, retains valid context and has a meaningful back path.

Evidence: 23 28 34 35 68

F04 · High

Make errors and missing data actionable

Several live pages show generic load failures. Missing advertising data and zero values are presented inconsistently across the overview and intelligence surfaces.

Why it matters: Users cannot distinguish no activity, no connected source and a failed request, or know how to recover.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Use separate states for Not connected, No records in this period, Loading and Could not load. Explain what remains available and give a relevant Retry, Connect source or Contact administrator action.

Check: Users correctly distinguish zero from unavailable data and can recover from a failed load without repeating a completed action.

Evidence: 01 05 12 19 25

F05 · High

Explain the relationship between imported and approved data

Community source inventory contains floorplans while the manual floorplan page is empty. Its empty message says to preview and confirm to archive the final plan. Imported text and approved facts also appear as separate stores without a clear bridge.

Why it matters: Users may assume property information disappeared or enter duplicate records.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Offer one understandable inventory with source and approval status. Separate imported, approved and manually maintained records visibly; provide review and adoption actions without silently approving existing imports.

Check: A user can explain why an imported item is not yet approved or published and can find its next review step.

Evidence: 03 04 07

F06 · High

Give the client portal a distinct, simpler purpose

Overview and Performance repeat much of their opening content. Insights reuses the internal analysis view. Code also shows that Insights navigation does not preserve query context in the same way as the other portal views.

Why it matters: Clients must choose between similar pages and interpret analyst terminology; property/date context may be lost when navigating.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Home should explain results and decisions. Results should contain trends and evidence. Reports should remain reviewed monthly narratives. Preserve property and date filters across views. Validate context preservation with a real client session before release.

Check: A client can explain the month, find the latest report and open a conversation without staff instruction; filters remain stable.

F07 · Medium

Apply one visual and interaction system

Button styles, accent colors, tab treatments and heading sizes vary between modules. Some workbenches look like default forms inside an otherwise polished shell.

Why it matters: The product feels assembled from separate tools and requires repeated learning.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Create shared page headers, tabs, tables, status badges, form sections, empty/error states and evidence drawers. Use a common type scale, spacing and primary action style.

Check: Comparable tasks use the same controls, labels and states across every module.

F08 · Medium

Prioritize the first mobile screen

At 390 px, navigation, introductions and controls consume most of the first screen. At 320 px, the client question field exceeds its card content area. The comparison table scrolls locally rather than causing whole-page overflow.

Why it matters: Users scroll before seeing useful information and may miss secondary tabs or controls.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Use a compact header, a single primary action, collapsible filters and card summaries for small screens. Keep table scrolling clearly signposted and make inputs fit their containers.

Check: At 320 and 390 px, the primary result/action appears early, fields stay inside cards and keyboard focus remains visible.

F09 · Medium

Improve MarketVision comparison and freshness

Competitor cards are useful, but historical brand paragraphs dominate and one brand-voice field renders a JSON-like array. Missing prices and old research compete with current values.

Why it matters: Users struggle to compare competitors and may mistake historical context for current market evidence.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Use consistent compact cards with current asking rent, observation date, availability and source. Put historical brand analysis in details. Format lists as readable tags and avoid unnecessary decimal precision.

Check: A user compares three competitors and correctly identifies which prices are current, missing or historical.

Evidence: 14 15 37 60 67

F10 · Medium

Translate operational language

The interface uses terms including text chunks, hash-guarded, Dead Letter, IANA and inquiry cohort stage ratios.

Why it matters: Nontechnical users need training to understand routine actions and may avoid them.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Use task language in primary views and reserve technical details for support/admin panels. Retain material limitations beside the relevant action in short, concrete wording.

Check: Users can describe each primary action in their own words and understand what will happen next.

Evidence: 04 07 08 16 20 22 27 31 33

F11 · Medium

Complete focused accessibility checks

The widget send button is icon-only and its source has no accessible name. The message input relies on placeholder text rather than an explicit label. Narrow layout issues were also observed.

Why it matters: Assistive-technology users may not understand controls, and zoomed/mobile layouts can become difficult to use.

Recommended change and acceptance check

Name the send button, label the message input and verify keyboard order, focus, dialogs, contrast, text resizing and target sizes across the shared components.

Check: A keyboard and screen-reader user can open chat, compose and send a message; a separate WCAG audit confirms the broader interface.

Scope and next validation

Reviewed

  • All primary staff navigation, selected detail screens and settings.
  • Six client portal sections, report and conversation detail.
  • Sign-in, recovery, mobile signup and the current visitor chat widget.
  • Targeted 390 px layouts and a 320 px client Insights check.
  • Targeted code review of shared components, navigation and copy.

Still to validate

  • Real client login, permissions, live data and persistent filter context.
  • Submissions, publication, invitations, message delivery and booking completion.
  • Every modal, role, property, empty/error state and public generated website.
  • Full keyboard, screen-reader, contrast and zoom review.
  • Two usability rounds with nontechnical staff and clients.

The local preview uses the current source components and styles, with a possible body-font difference from production. The extra preview banner is not part of the product. Original captures can contain internal workspace information; keep this review within the intended audience.
